Pet Adoptions in Australia

Explore the patterns, challenges and changing landscape of pet adoption across Australia.

What Does 'Adoption' Really Mean?

Many people use “adopting a pet” to describe bringing any new furry friend home. But there’s a difference between owning a pet and adopting one through a shelter or rescue organisation.

The vast majority of pets are purchased directly through private breeders or commercial pet stores, with formal shelter adoptions accounting for just 36.3% of acquisitions nationwide.

So where across the country are formal adoptions actually thriving?

Where Are Adoptions Most Common?

To make a fair comparison, adoption numbers are measured against each state's resident population, rather than just looking at the total number of adoptions, giving a more accurate picture of regional differences across Australia.

Victoria leads the country in adoption rates relative to its population, followed by Queensland and New South Wales.

But where are the rescue organisations that help make these adoptions possible?

Mapping Australia's Rescue Network

The numbers reveal how rescue organisations are spread across Australia, with some states having far more active organisations than others. These organisations play an important role in connecting pets in need with people looking to adopt.

New South Wales has the highest number of active rescue organisations, followed by Victoria and Queensland. These are the same three states with the highest adoption rates relative to population, highlighting a potential link between rescue networks and adoption activity.

Which Pets Are Adopted Most?

Cats and dogs make up the largest share of pet adoptions, with other animals, including birds and guinea pigs, make up a much smaller proportion of adoptions, but how do their stories unfold once they enter the rehoming process?

Shelter Outcome Trends

The data offers encouraging news: rehoming is the most common outcome for shelter animals, with 64% of cats finding new homes and low return rates overall (1–5%). Additionally, 20% of dogs are successfully reunited with their original owners.

Why Do People Adopt Pets?

Regardless of age, companionship remains the single overwhelmingly dominant reason Australians seek pet adoption, holding consistently near 70% across all age brackets.

Barriers to Adoption Consideration

A desire for specific breeds or young puppies/kittens remains the primary hesitation preventing prospective pet owners from choosing shelter adoptions.

Concerns about potential behavioural issues are more common among those considering dogs, while negative past experiences with cats are a more prominent barrier for cat adoption.

Addressing the Adoption Decline

Following years of remarkably high successful adoption rates—hovering around 95%—data from 2025 reveals a sharp downturn in shelter placements across all pets.
