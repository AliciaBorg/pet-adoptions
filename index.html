What “Adoption” Actually Means

A common misconception is that simply aquiring a pet counts as adoption, including when buying from a commercial breeder or pet shop. But actually, adoption has a specific meaning: giving a surrendered or rescued animal a new home.

In fact, formal adoptions typically represent a minority of pet acquisitions. Survey results from 2024 show that just 36.2% of pets were formally adopted through a shelter, rescue or adoption agency.

Where Adoptions Are Happening in Australia

With formal adoptions accounting for a minority of pet acquisitions, where these adoptions are actually happening in Australia?

In 2025, Victoria recorded the highest PetRescue adoption rate, followed by Queensland and New South Wales.

This begs the question: what's driving these geographical differences?

In 2025, PetRescue member organisations are heavily concentrated along Australia's eastern states, being Victoria, Queensland and New South Wales.

They are also the states with the highest adoption rates, suggesting that rescue organisation distribution could play a role in adoption rates.

To understand these patterns more closely, let's zoom in on one state and see where these member organisations are located.

Victoria's 163 PetRescue member organisation locations are heavily concentrated around metropolitan Melbourne, with others spread across regional centres and towns.

But geographical patterns only tell part of the story. To better understand adoption in Australia, it is also important to look at which animals are finding homes.

Who Is Being Adopted?

In 2025, dogs and cats accounted for the vast majority of recorded PetRescue adoptions, while other species made up a much smaller share.

For this reason, the following sections will largely focus on dogs and cats, exploring what influences their waiting times and shapes adopter decisions.

Dogs and Cats: What Influences How Long They Wait?

With dogs and cats accounting for the large majority of adoptions, what determines how quickly they find a home?

Several factors may shape how long dogs and cats wait for a home, from the type of organisation listing them to their age and size.

Shelters offer the fastest pathway for both species, with dogs and cats waiting roughly the same amount of time.
Senior dogs and cats wait significantly longer than younger animals, while cats consistently move through adoption faster than dogs.
For dogs, longer waits generally accompany larger size tiers, while cats consistently have shorter adoption times across every tier.

What Shapes Adopter Decisions?

Waiting times reflect not only animal characteristics, but also adopter preferences. So, what makes people hesitate to adopt a pet?

A 2025 survey found that preferring a young puppy or kitten and wanting a specific breed were the most commonly reported barriers for both dogs and cats.

On the bright side, a 2024 survey found that around 70% of respondents across all age groups cited companionship as a reason to adopt, highlighting the shared desire for connection with a pet across generations.

While companionship remains a strong motivation for adoption, are these intentions translating into successful outcomes?

Recent Rapid Changes in the Adoption Landscape.

Between January and December 2025, the share of PetRescue listings resulting in adoption fell sharply across dogs, cats and all pets, suggesting a widespread decline in Australia.

With a smaller share of listed pets finding homes, understanding these trends is increasingly important to helping more rescue animals get adopted.
