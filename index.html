Introduction

What is adoption?

Adoption means giving a pet from a shelter or rescue organisation a new home. It is not the same as buying a pet from a commercial breeder, pet shop or online broker, even though these are all ways Australians acquire companion animals.

That distinction matters: formal adoption is one specific pathway within the wider pet market, and it remains a minority pathway for people who own pets.

The waffle chart makes that difference visible: of every 100 pets acquired in 2024, only 36 came through a shelter or adoption agency.

What influences how long pets wait?

Geography determines where adoption can happen. But once a pet is listed, age, size and the type of organisation presenting it can shape how quickly it finds a home.

The thread

Make the right pet easier to find.

Adoption outcomes reflect more than animal characteristics. They also reflect visibility, local rescue capacity and the expectations people bring to the search. Better matching, broader storytelling about older and larger animals, and stronger support for rescue organisations could help turn more listings into lasting homes.

Data shown is based on the supplied PetRescue and publicly available Australian datasets. Rates and counts describe the periods noted on each chart and should not be interpreted as a complete census of every Australian rescue organisation.
